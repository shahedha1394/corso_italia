This page is for you if you have **never applied to a university abroad** and you don't know where to start. Go through it slowly, one step at a time. You don't need to understand everything today.

The other guides on this site give the full details. Here we only explain **what each step means** and **what to do now**.

## Before you start: three things to know

1. **Start early.** Some deadlines, such as university applications and scholarships, close months before classes begin.
2. **Always trust the official page.** Rules and dates can change every year. The university's website, Universitaly and the Italian embassy are the sources that count. Our guides link to them.
3. **The official portal is free.** Pre-enrolment on [Universitaly](https://www.universitaly.it) costs nothing. Be careful with anyone who asks you for money to do it for you.

## Words you will see often

- **Bachelor's degree** (*laurea triennale*): the first university degree, after secondary school.
- **Master's degree** (*laurea magistrale*): the degree after a bachelor's.
- **Universitaly**: the official Italian website where non-EU students fill in their pre-enrolment.
- **CIMEA statement** or **Declaration of Value** (*Dichiarazione di valore*): a document that shows what your diploma or degree is worth in Italy.
- **DSU**: the regional scholarship. It can give you money, and sometimes a room and meals.
- **Study visa (type D)**: the visa you need to enter Italy to study.
- **Residence permit** (*permesso di soggiorno*): the document that lets you stay in Italy after you arrive.
- **Codice fiscale**: your Italian tax code. You need it for many things, like buying a SIM card.

## Step 1: Decide what you want to study, and in which language

Ask yourself two questions:

- **What level?** A bachelor's (after secondary school) or a master's (after a bachelor's)?
- **What subject?** Write down two or three subjects you like.

Then think about the language. Many programs are taught in **English**, others in **Italian**. Both usually ask for at least level **B2** in that language.

**Do now:** write on a sheet of paper: the level, two or three subjects, and the language you can study in.

If your English is not strong yet, [EngPro](../../engpro/) offers free English lessons with volunteer teachers.

Read more: [Language requirements](../language-requirements/)

## Step 2: Find programs and universities

There are three easy places to look:

1. **Universitaly** ([universitaly.it](https://www.universitaly.it)): its official search covers all the programs in Italy.
2. **Our [university guide](../universities/)** and the [map of cities](../cities/): each university page links to its official list of programs.
3. **The university's own website.** Once you find a program you like, open its page on the university site.

**How to search on Google (tips):**

- Write in **English**, for example: *master's degree in economics taught in English Italy*.
- Add the name of the university and *international students admission*, for example: *University of Bologna international students admission*.
- Open the **university's own website**, not a random blog or video. Information on unofficial pages is often old.

**Do now:** make a simple table with these columns: *program name, university, city, language, deadline, link*. Add every program you like. Three to five programs is a good start.

## Step 3: Check that you meet the requirements

On each program page, look for the section called *admission requirements* (or *requisiti di accesso* in Italian). Usually:

- For a **bachelor's**: a secondary school certificate after at least **12 years** of school.
- For a **master's**: a bachelor's degree, and your transcript (the list of your exams and grades).
- A **language certificate**, often at **B2** level.
- Some programs have an **entrance exam** or special admission rules, for example Architecture or Medicine.

**Do now:** for each program in your table, write *yes* or *no*: do I meet the requirements?

Read more: [How to apply](../how-to-apply/) and [Admission tests](../admission-tests/)

## Step 4: Prepare your documents

Start this early, because some documents take time. Usually you need:

- Your **passport**.
- Your **diploma** or degree, and for a master's your **transcript**.
- A **CIMEA statement** or a **Declaration of Value** from the Italian embassy.
- **Translations** where needed.
- Your **language certificate**.

**Do now:** make a folder on your phone or computer and scan every document into it. Give each file a clear name.

Read more: [How to apply](../how-to-apply/), section 2

## Step 5: Think about money and scholarships

Studying in Italy has costs: university fees, rent, food. For the visa you must also **show that you have enough money**.

The good news: there are scholarships. The biggest one for most students is the **regional DSU scholarship**. It depends on your family's income. Its deadlines are often **before classes start**, so check them as soon as you choose your university.

**Do now:** open the scholarship finder on the [funding page](../funding/), answer the questions and see which scholarships may fit you.

Read more: [Scholarships and funding](../funding/) and [How much it costs](../costs/)

## Step 6: Apply to the university, then on Universitaly

This happens in two parts:

1. **Apply to the university**, usually through its own online portal, before its deadline. The university checks your documents and decides.
2. **Fill in your pre-enrolment on Universitaly.** The university then **validates** it online. Without a validated pre-enrolment, you cannot apply for a study visa.

**Do now:** write the application deadline of each university in your table, and put a reminder in your phone's calendar.

Read more: [How to apply](../how-to-apply/), sections 3 and 4

## Step 7: Apply for your study visa

With your validated pre-enrolment, you apply at the **Italian embassy or consulate in the country where you live**. You need a **type D study visa**. Book your appointment early.

Read more: [Study visa](../visa/)

## Step 8: When you arrive in Italy

Welcome! In the first days you have a few important tasks:

- Apply for your **residence permit** within **8 working days** of arriving.
- Finish your **enrolment** at the university.
- Get your **codice fiscale**, a phone number and a place to live.

Read more: [Your first weeks in Italy](../../living/first-weeks/) and [Residence permit](../../living/residence-permit/)
