**Important.** All of these services are free or nearly free, but opening hours and conditions change. Call or write before you go. Most of them help everyone, whatever your nationality or religion. Corso Italy is not linked to any of these organisations.

**Where to start:** your university's international office. If you don't know where to go for anything else, go to a **Caritas listening centre** (*Centro di ascolto*) in your city: they listen to your situation and send you to the right service.

## 1. Legal help

### Residence permit and paperwork
- **Patronati:** offices linked to the trade unions, such as INCA (CGIL), INAS (CISL), ACLI and ITAL (UIL). They help with the residence permit and its renewal, family reunification, citizenship and the ISEE. Many of their services are free: ask before you start. Call first, because many work by appointment.
- **Immigration desks of the city** (*Sportello immigrazione*): many cities have one, for example Torino Migranti in Turin. The City of Milan also offers free legal guidance online, by appointment on 02.02.02.

### Lawyers and specialised organisations
- **ASGI** (Association for Legal Studies on Immigration): lawyers who specialise in immigration, asylum and discrimination. Its lawyers follow the cases of students from Gaza. Website: asgi.it.
- **CIR** (Italian Refugee Council), Rome: guidance and legal assistance for refugees and stateless people. Website: cir-onlus.org.
- **Centro Astalli:** legal and social help for refugees and asylum seekers, in Rome, Bologna, Catania, Palermo, Grumo Nevano, Trento, Vicenza and Padova.
- **Naga** (Milan): free legal, health and social help for foreigners, asylum seekers and refugees. Phone 02 58102599, website naga.it.
- **Avvocato di strada:** volunteer lawyers who give free legal help to people without a home, with offices across Italy. Website: avvocatodistrada.it.
- **University legal clinics** (*cliniche legali*): law students with professors and lawyers give free help, especially on migration (for example at the University of Palermo).

### Inside your university
- **Student ombudsman** (*Garante degli studenti* or *Difensore civico*): for problems with the university itself, such as fees, delays or how you were treated. At Padova, for example, you write to difensore.civico@unipd.it.

### Discrimination
- **UNAR** (National Office against Racial Discrimination): free phone number **800 90 10 10**, website unar.it.

### If you need a lawyer in court
- **State-funded legal aid** (*patrocinio a spese dello Stato*): if your taxable income is up to **€13,659.64 a year**, the State pays your lawyer. Foreigners with a regular residence permit can ask for it. You apply at the bar association (*Consiglio dell'Ordine degli avvocati*) of your city.

## 2. Health

- **National Health Service (SSN):** as a student you can enrol voluntarily for **€700 a calendar year** and get a family doctor. See our guide on [health insurance](../health-insurance/).
- **STP code** (for people without a residence permit): it gives you urgent and essential care in public hospitals and clinics. It is free and anonymous, valid for 6 months and renewable, and **using it is not reported to the police**. Ask at the local health office (ASL) or a hospital.
- **EMERGENCY:** free care for people in need. Clinics in Marghera (Venice), Rosarno, Castel Volturno, Naples Ponticelli and Sassari, mobile clinics in Milan and Ragusa, and a help desk in Brescia. In Milan, a free social help desk travels around the city (book on 02 86316890).
- **Opera San Francesco** (Milan): a free clinic with general, specialist and dental care and medicines, for people without access to the National Health Service.
- **MEDU** (Doctors for Human Rights): a mobile clinic in Rome for forced migrants, with basic care and referrals to mental health services.
- **Centro Astalli** (Rome): the SaMiFo health service, with special attention to mental health and to survivors of torture.
- **Naga** (Milan): free health care for foreigners.
- **Your university:** most universities offer free psychological counselling to their students.

## 3. Food and daily needs

- **Caritas:** present in almost every city, through the dioceses. Start at the **listening centre** (*Centro di ascolto*). Depending on the city, Caritas offers meals, food parcels, clothes, and **solidarity shops** (*empori solidali*) where you take food for free with a points card.
- **Opera San Francesco** (Milan): free meals at lunch and dinner, showers, and clothes every four weeks. First go to the reception desk (via Kramer 1 or via Antonello da Messina 4) to get a card, which you renew every four months.
- **Pane Quotidiano** (Milan): free food parcels on Viale Toscana and Viale Monza, without proof of income (a first registration may be asked).
- **Centro Astalli** (Rome): a canteen for asylum seekers and refugees.
- **University canteens:** low prices, and free meals for students with a regional scholarship (DSU).

## 4. Housing and Italian classes

- **Housing:**
  - Centro Astalli runs reception centres and cohousing for refugees.
  - **Refugees Welcome Italia** matches refugees with families who host them for free.
  - In Rome, the Diocese with the Community of Sant'Egidio hosts students from Gaza.
  - Caritas helps with housing in some cities.
- **Free Italian:**
  - **Sant'Egidio Schools of Language and Culture**: free courses for foreigners in Rome, and schools in Milan, Genoa, Naples, Florence and other cities.
  - **CPIA**: public schools for adults (16 and over), with a 200-hour Italian course to reach level A2. Ask the school about any costs.
  - Centro Astalli's Italian school, and your university's language centre.

## 5. Notes for Palestinian students, before you leave

- **Legal help must start while you are still in Palestine.** The hardest problems come before departure: exit permission, the visa and documents. ASGI lawyers follow these cases. In February 2026 a court in Rome ordered visas to be issued for a family from Gaza.
- **Documents:** check that your passport is valid, and get your certificates translated, legalised or assessed by CIMEA **early**. If your university was destroyed and you cannot get documents, tell the Italian university at once.
- **Money for the visa:** admission alone is not enough. You need to show €10,179.85 a year, through a scholarship or a guarantor.
- **Family:** evacuations do not include family members. According to L'Espresso, Jordan usually lets only children under 18 months travel with student mothers. Family reunification is possible only after you get your residence permit in Italy.
- **After you arrive:** ask for legal advice **before** you decide to apply for asylum or to stay with a study permit, because each choice has different effects.

See also our guides on [Palestinian students](../../study/palestinian-students/) and [scholarships for refugees and students from conflict areas](../../study/refugees-and-conflict-areas/).

## 6. Good to know

- **Deadlines to appeal are short.** If you receive a refusal of your residence permit or any official decision, go to a patronato or a lawyer **at once**.
- **Keep copies** of all your documents: photos on your phone, and a copy with someone you trust.
- **Do not rent without a registered contract** (*in nero*): you need it to renew your residence permit and for scholarships. See [finding accommodation](../accommodation/).
- **Caritas helps Italians and foreigners alike**, and most of these services help everyone.
- **Ask your university first:** many universities have emergency funds, psychological support and international offices that students don't know about.
